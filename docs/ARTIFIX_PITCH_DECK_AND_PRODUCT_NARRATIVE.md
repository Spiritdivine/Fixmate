# SECTION 1: THE STORY — HOW BROKEN TRUST & "CASH ADVANCES" DESTROY HOMES AND WORKERS

### The Protagonist: Meet Amaka & Ibrahim

#### The Homeowner: Amaka
Amaka is a 31-year-old working professional living in Lekki, Lagos. She recently moved into a new apartment with her young daughter. On a rainy Tuesday evening, a major electrical surge blows her distribution box, leaving her home in pitch-black darkness, spoiling food in the fridge, and posing an immediate electrical fire hazard.

Like **tens of millions of urban residents across Africa**, Amaka does not have an engineer on speed dial. She asks around her neighborhood for an electrician's phone number. An unverified phone number is passed along by a security guard.

#### The Skilled Artisan: Ibrahim
Across town in Surulere lives Ibrahim. Ibrahim is a talented, hardworking certified electrician with eight years of experience. He takes pride in his craft, owns his safety tools, and wants to build an honest livelihood for his family.

Yet, Ibrahim is exhausted. Two weeks ago, a commercial building owner had Ibrahim rewire an entire duplex. When Ibrahim finished the job, the owner stopped answering his calls, claiming: *"My managing director hasn’t signed off on the funds; check back next month."* Ibrahim was left having to borrow money just to feed his family and pay for his transport.

---

### The Incident: How the Traditional Artisan System Breaks Down

When Amaka calls the electrician she was recommended, she immediately steps into the classic, stressful **trap of informal hiring**:

#### Trap A: The "Disappearing Advance" Trap
1. **The Assessment**: The electrician arrives, looks at the burnt board, and says: *"Aunty, the whole main circuit breaker and copper cables are burnt. Give me ₦120,000 upfront right now to go to the electrical market to buy original materials, plus ₦30,000 for my transport and initial work."*
2. **The Handover**: In panic, needing light for her daughter and food preservation, Amaka transfers ₦150,000 directly to the electrician's personal bank account.
3. **The Disappearance**: The electrician leaves for the market. Two hours pass. Five hours pass. Night falls.
4. **The Ghosting**: Amaka calls his line: *"The number you are calling is currently switched off."* He never returns. Her ₦150,000 is gone, her house remains in dangerous darkness, and she has zero recourse, zero address, and no formal contract.

#### Trap B: Substandard Materials & The Fire Hazard
1. When another worker finally shows up, he cuts corners to maximize his personal profit margin. He purchases cheap, counterfeit aluminum-clad wires instead of pure copper, pockets the price difference, and patches the wall with quick plaster.
2. Two weeks later, the breaker trips, sparks fly, and a dangerous wall fire ignites. Amaka has to spend triple the amount repairing the damage.

#### Trap C: The Diaspora "Ghost House" Dilemma
1. Amaka’s brother, Chidi, lives in London. He works long hours in the UK NHS and sends £1,500 every month back home to build a family home in Enugu.
2. He sends the funds to a trusted local supervisor who promises the foundation, plumbing, and roofing are moving smoothly.
3. A year later, a visiting friend visits the site: there is no roof, the plumbing pipes are nonexistent, and the money was diverted into personal expenses. Chidi has no timestamped proof, no accountability, and no peace of mind.
